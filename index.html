How false alarms are controlled
A Sentinel-1 drop must persist for months. Each patch is then checked by independent evidence: NISAR L-band (HV far below intact forest), Sentinel-2 vegetation loss (NDVI), and RADD alerts. High = at least two agree. Rivers and permanent water are masked; patches on steep ground or floodplain are flagged for review. Nothing is claimed to be 100 % — see the validation numbers below.
Why L-band? (physics)
NISAR's L-band (24 cm) passes through leaves and scatters from branches and trunks. Cross-pol HV is therefore a proxy for woody biomass: when forest is cut, HV collapses. Co-pol HH can bounce off water and tree trunks (double-bounce), so flooded forest turns bright while open water turns dark — the “breathing” of the Amazon floodplain. C-band (Sentinel-1) mostly sees the canopy top, so the two sensors complement each other.
Radar alerts are candidate clearings — confirm with optical and field data.
